INTRODUCTION

Agriculture in Southern Punjab faces serious challenges from soil salinity, poor groundwater quality, and limited irrigation water, which affect crop productivity and efficient resource use. Satellite imagery provides valuable information about field conditions, but surface observations alone cannot fully explain root-zone salinity or groundwater-related risks. The proposed Explainable AI Based Farming Assistant Mobile Application aims to combine satellite observations with groundwater, weather, soil, and crop information to estimate yield, assess salt-leaching requirements, support fertilizer timing, and provide understandable, field-level recommendations for farmers.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Agriculture in Southern Punjab is under stress from soil salinity, poor quality of groundwater and limited irrigation water supplies, which can impact crop yields and result in increased loss of fertilizer. Farmers need practical guidance on whether salt leaching is suitable, how much water may be required, and when fertilizer should be applied. This project will develop an explainable AI mobile application using satellite imagery, available groundwater-quality records, weather data, and crop information to estimate salinity risk and crop yield. It will recommend suitable salt-leaching and fertilizer-timing scenarios, explain the reasons behind its recommendations, and highlight uncertainty when reliable data are unavailable.

Gaps

  • Root-zone limitation: Optical satellite data mainly observe surface soil (0–5 cm), not the 0–60 cm active root zone.
  • Groundwater-quality gap: Water models focus on quantity, with limited direct integration of groundwater salinity (EC/SAR).
  • Leaching-water gap: FAO CROPWAT estimates crop water needs but does not automatically add salt-leaching requirements.
  • Farmer advisory gap: Salinity and yield predictions are rarely translated into practical, explainable recommendations.
  • Partial system coverage: Existing tools emphasize weather or monitoring; advanced salinity and groundwater optimization remain uneven.
  • Missing end-to-end solution: None of the eight compared systems fully combine soil-water quality, explainable salinity and yield prediction, and actionable guidance.

CONCLUSION

This project will develop an explainable AI-based mobile decision-support application integrating satellite imagery, available groundwater-quality, climate, and crop data to estimate soil salinity risks and crop yield, and recommend salt-leaching and fertilizer timing scenarios. Rather than relying on costly hardware sensors, the system will provide transparent guidance by explaining the reasons behind its recommendations and highlighting uncertainty whenever reliable groundwater records are unavailable.

Ultimately, the proposed system will help farmers transition toward data-driven input management, enabling them to optimize scarce freshwater resources, reduce fertilizer burn, and protect crop yields. It will also assist agricultural extension experts in delivering timely advisory services and equip researchers with a unified tool to study soil-water salinization dynamics across arid and semi-arid environments.
